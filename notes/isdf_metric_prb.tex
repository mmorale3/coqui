\title{Coulomb-metric point selection in interpolative separable density fitting}

\author{Miguel A. Morales}
\affiliation{Center for Computational Quantum Physics, Flatiron Institute, New York, NY 10010, USA}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
The interpolative separable density fitting (ISDF) representation of the
two-electron Coulomb integrals underpins low-scaling implementations of
hybrid density-functional theory, many-body perturbation theory, and
quantum-simulation resource estimation. The efficiency of the representation
is governed by the number of interpolation points per orbital,
$\cmu = \Nmu/N_{\rm orb}$, required to reach a target accuracy:
auxiliary-space storage scales as $O(\Nmu^{2})$ and dense auxiliary-space
linear algebra as $O(\Nmu^{3})$. Interpolation points are conventionally
selected by a pivoted Cholesky factorization of the orbital-pair overlap
matrix, an objective that weights all Fourier components of the pair
densities equally and consequently over-resolves short-wavelength structure
that the Coulomb interaction suppresses. Starting from the observation that
the grid-space metric cancels exactly from the ISDF least-squares problem,
so that any metric can influence the factorization only through the choice
of interpolation points and through pair-space weights, we introduce three
composable modifications of the point-selection procedure: (i) a separable
weighting of the selection Gram matrix in the space of orbital pairs,
(ii) point selection on Gaussian-filtered surrogate orbitals, and (iii) an
exact Coulomb-metric re-ranking of an enlarged candidate pool generated by
the overlap-metric factorization. None of these modifications alters the
ISDF ansatz, the interpolation-vector equations, or any downstream consumer
of the factorization, and each reduces to the conventional algorithm in a
well-defined limit. For bulk silicon described by norm-conserving,
ultrasoft, and projector augmented-wave pseudopotentials, we quantify the
error in the Hartree, exact-exchange, and random-phase-approximation
correlation energies as a function of $\cmu$
\todo{and find: summary of $\cmu^{*}$ reductions, per scheme and composed}.
The re-ranking step increases the cost of point selection by a factor
$s \approx 2$ without changing its asymptotic scaling, and point selection
remains a negligible fraction of the total cost of the calculation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Low-scaling formulations of electronic-structure methods beyond
density-functional theory rely on compressed representations of the
two-electron repulsion integrals. Among these, the interpolative separable
density fitting (ISDF), or tensor hypercontraction (THC), representation
factorizes the orbital-pair densities through a set of real-space
interpolation points,
\begin{equation}
  \rho_p(\rr) \equiv u^{*}_{i}(\rr)\, u_{a}(\rr)
  \;\approx\; \sum_{\mu=1}^{\Nmu} \zeta_\mu(\rr)\, \rho_p(\rr_\mu),
  \label{eq:isdf}
\end{equation}
and thereby reduces the four-index Coulomb tensor to products of two-index
quantities \todo{cite: Lu--Ying 2015; Hu--Lin--Yang 2017;
Parrish--Hohenstein--Mart\'inez THC; CoQui}. The representation has been
employed in Hartree--Fock and hybrid-functional exchange, M{\o}ller--Plesset
and random-phase-approximation (RPA) correlation energies, coupled-cluster
theory, $GW$ and related many-body perturbation frameworks, and in resource
estimates for fault-tolerant quantum simulation \todo{cites}. In all of
these applications the auxiliary (interpolation-point) index carries the
factorization: every object stored on that index requires $O(\Nmu^{2})$
memory, and every factorization or inversion in the auxiliary space requires
$O(\Nmu^{3})$ operations. The proportionality constant
$\cmu = \Nmu/N_{\rm orb}$ at fixed target accuracy is therefore the central
figure of merit of the method; a reduction of $\cmu$ from 10 to 6
corresponds to a factor of $2.8$ in auxiliary-space storage and $4.6$ in
dense auxiliary-space linear algebra, uniformly across applications.

In the standard algorithm the interpolation points are selected by a
pivoted Cholesky factorization of the Gram matrix of orbital-pair densities,
which greedily minimizes the $L^{2}$ error of the fitted pair densities on
the real-space grid \todo{cite}. This objective is optimal for the pair
densities themselves but not for the quantities the factorization is used to
compute: every consumer of the representation contracts it against the
Coulomb kernel $v(\GG) = 4\pi/|\GG|^{2}$, or a screened variant thereof,
which suppresses short-wavelength errors by two powers of $|\GG|$. Points
selected in the overlap metric therefore allocate resolution to features of
the pair densities that contribute negligibly to Coulomb matrix elements.

In this work we adapt the point selection---and only the point
selection---to the Coulomb metric. The analysis begins from an exact
structural property of the ISDF least-squares problem, established in
Sec.~\ref{sec:cancellation}: the interpolation vectors are independent of
any grid-space metric, which cancels identically from the normal equations.
All freedom therefore resides in the choice of interpolation points and in
pair-space weights. We exploit this freedom through three independent and
mutually compatible modifications of the selection procedure
(Sec.~\ref{sec:theory}), all of which reuse the existing pivoted-Cholesky
machinery, are controlled from the program input, and reduce to the standard
algorithm in well-defined limits. Section~\ref{sec:results} quantifies the
resulting errors in the Hartree, exact-exchange, and RPA correlation
energies of bulk silicon for norm-conserving, ultrasoft, and projector
augmented-wave (PAW) pseudopotentials.

\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{Exact cancellation of the grid metric}
\label{sec:cancellation}

Let the pair densities be collected on a real-space grid in the matrix
$Z \in \mathbb{C}^{N_g \times N_{\rm pair}}$, $Z_{gp} = \rho_p(\rr_g)$, and
let $\Theta = Z[I,:]$ denote its restriction to a set of interpolation
points $I = \{\rr_\mu\}$. The ISDF ansatz~\eqref{eq:isdf} reads
$Z \approx \zeta\,\Theta$ with $\zeta \in \mathbb{C}^{N_g\times \Nmu}$.
Consider the weighted least-squares functional
\begin{equation}
  E \;=\; \mathrm{Tr}\!\left[ (Z-\zeta\Theta)\, W\, (Z-\zeta\Theta)^{\dagger}\, v \right],
  \label{eq:functional}
\end{equation}
with an arbitrary Hermitian positive-definite grid metric $v$ and a
diagonal pair-space weight $W = \mathrm{diag}(w_p)$, $w_p \ge 0$. The
stationarity condition
$v\,(Z-\zeta\Theta)\,W\,\Theta^{\dagger} = 0$, together with the
invertibility of $v$, yields
\begin{equation}
  \zeta \;=\; Z\,W\,\Theta^{\dagger} \left( \Theta\, W\, \Theta^{\dagger} \right)^{-1}.
  \label{eq:zeta}
\end{equation}
The grid metric cancels identically, whereas the pair weight does not. The
cancellation has a simple structural origin: $\zeta$ carries an unconstrained
grid index, so the residual is driven orthogonal to the row space of
$\Theta$ at each grid point independently, and no reweighting across grid
points can improve upon this. Three consequences inform all subsequent
developments. First, the interpolation vectors produced by the standard
algorithm are already optimal in \emph{any} grid metric for the given point
set; there is nothing to correct in the solve. Second, no
alternating-optimization or higher-scaling refinement of $\zeta$ at fixed
$I$ can lower the error in any grid metric. Third, a metric can enter the
factorization only through the selection of $I$ and through the pair weights
$W$; both mechanisms are inexpensive, and they define the three schemes
introduced below.

\subsection{The selection functional}
\label{sec:objective}

With $\zeta$ eliminated through Eq.~\eqref{eq:zeta}, the fit error in the
metric $v$ becomes a function of the point set alone,
\begin{equation}
  E_v(I) \;=\; \mathrm{Tr}\left[\, Q_\perp\, M \,\right],
  \qquad M = Z^{\dagger} v\, Z,
  \label{eq:objective}
\end{equation}
where $Q_\perp$ projects onto the orthogonal complement, in pair space, of
the rows of $\Theta$. Greedy minimization of Eq.~\eqref{eq:objective} is
equivalent to pivoted Cholesky factorization of
\begin{equation}
  K \;=\; Z\, M\, Z^{\dagger} \;=\; C\, v\, C, \qquad C = Z Z^{\dagger},
  \label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
in which $C_{gg'} = A_{gg'}\, B^{*}_{gg'}$ inherits the Hadamard structure
of a product of orbital Gram matrices,
$A_{gg'} = \sum_i u_i(\rr_g)u_i^{*}(\rr_{g'})$; this structure renders the
diagonal and individual columns of $C$ computable in $O(N_g N_{\rm orb})$
operations. The conventional selection corresponds to the replacement
$M \rightarrow \mathbb{1}$, i.e., to pivoting on $C$ itself. Direct
pivoting on $K$ is not affordable: the Coulomb kernel is a convolution and
does not commute with the Hadamard structure, so that a single column of
$K$ costs $O(N_g N_i N_a)$ and a complete selection
$O(N_g N_{\rm orb}^{3})$. The three schemes below approximate or realize
the $v$-weighted objective at essentially the cost of the standard
algorithm.

\subsection{Pair-space weighting}
\label{sec:knob1}

The pair weight $W$ of Eq.~\eqref{eq:functional} survives the elimination of
$\zeta$ and modifies the selection functional directly. A general weight
$w_{ia}$ would destroy the Hadamard factorization that makes the selection
affordable, but any weight expressible as a short sum of separable
nonnegative terms,
\begin{equation}
  w_{ia} \;=\; \sum_{p=1}^{N_L} c_p\, f_p(\varepsilon_i)\, g_p(\varepsilon_a),
  \qquad c_p, f_p, g_p \ge 0,
  \label{eq:sepweight}
\end{equation}
preserves it at $N_L$ times the cost: each term contributes a Gram matrix of
scaled orbitals, and the weighted selection matrix
$C^{w} = \sum_p c_p\, A^{(p)} \odot B^{(p)*}$ remains positive semidefinite
by the Schur product theorem, so the pivoted factorization retains its
numerical robustness. In practice the weights are functions of the
single-particle energies. Energy-denominator weights,
$w_{ia} = (|\varepsilon_i - \mu| + |\varepsilon_a - \mu| + \eta)^{-1}$,
which reduce to the familiar $1/(\varepsilon_a-\varepsilon_i+\eta)$ for
occupied--virtual pairs, admit a Laplace expansion into the form of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:sepweight} with all coefficients nonnegative and no numerical
overflow; quadrature orders $N_L = 4$--$6$ suffice, since the pivot ordering
is a discrete choice far more forgiving than a continuous fit. Beyond fixed
functional forms, Eq.~\eqref{eq:sepweight} defines a design space: a rank-1
weight ($N_L = 1$) costs no more than the unweighted selection, and the
parameters of a simple family $f(\varepsilon)$ can be optimized directly
against the error in the target observables. The weight is applied to the
point selection only; the interpolation vectors are always obtained from
Eq.~\eqref{eq:zeta} with $W = \mathbb{1}$, so a single point set can serve
several consumers. Unweighted selection depends only on the span of the
orbital set and is therefore invariant under unitary rotations within it;
energy-dependent weights forfeit this invariance, and the point set should
then be selected once and reused.

\subsection{Filtered-orbital surrogate}
\label{sec:knob2}

The second scheme performs the point selection on attenuated surrogate
orbitals whose plane-wave coefficients are damped by a Gaussian,
\begin{equation}
  \tilde c_{n\kk}(\GG) = c_{n\kk}(\GG)\,
  e^{-\alpha |\kk+\GG|^{2} / G_c^{2}},
  \label{eq:filter}
\end{equation}
where $G_c$ denotes the wave-function cutoff. The selection Gram matrix is
assembled from the $\tilde u$, the pivoted factorization selects the
points, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:zeta} is subsequently solved with the
\emph{unfiltered} orbitals; only the point set is retained from the
surrogate. Because the Fourier transform of a pair density is a convolution
of the transforms of its factors, filtering both factors attenuates the
product at an effective width reduced by $\sqrt{2}$ and with a Gaussian
rather than $1/G^{2}$ profile; the scheme is therefore a surrogate for,
rather than an implementation of, the Coulomb metric. Its appeal is
practical: the cost is a single additional Fourier-transform sweep during
selection, and the limit $\alpha = 0$ recovers the standard algorithm
exactly.

\subsection{Coulomb-metric re-ranking of a candidate pool}
\label{sec:knob3}

The third scheme realizes the Coulomb-metric objective exactly on a
restricted search space. The standard (or weighted, or filtered) pivoted
factorization is first carried to an enlarged rank
$N_1 = \lceil s \Nmu \rceil$ with $s \in [1.5,3]$, producing a candidate
pool $P$ and the Cholesky factor $L \in \mathbb{C}^{N_g\times N_1}$.
Because the Schur complement vanishes identically on eliminated pivots, the
restriction of the Gram matrix to the pool is reproduced exactly by the
factor, $C[P,:] = \ell\, L^{\dagger}$ with $\ell = L[P,:]$ lower
triangular. Consequently,
\begin{equation}
  K_{PP} \;=\; \ell\, \left(L^{\dagger} v L\right) \ell^{\dagger}
        \;=\; Y^{\dagger} Y,
  \qquad Y = R\,\ell^{\dagger},
  \label{eq:KPP}
\end{equation}
where $R(\GG,j) = \sqrt{\bar v(\GG)}\, \hat L(\GG,j)$ is assembled from the
Fourier transforms of the columns of $L$ and a metric multiplier
$\bar v(\GG)$, for which we consider the bare kernel, a Gaussian-attenuated
kernel, and Brillouin-zone averages thereof. A dense pivoted Cholesky
factorization of the $N_1 \times N_1$ matrix $K_{PP}$ then re-ranks the
pool in the Coulomb metric, and the leading $\Nmu$ points are retained.
The Coulomb-optimal subset is sought only within the overlap-selected pool;
this restriction is what renders the procedure affordable, at a cost of
approximately a factor $s$ on the selection step with no change in
asymptotic scaling. The limits $s = 1$ and $\bar v = 1$ reproduce the
pool and its ordering exactly.

\subsection{Implementation}
\label{sec:implementation}

The three schemes are implemented in the CoQui code \todo{cite} within the
existing pivoted-Cholesky selection kernels and are controlled entirely
from the program input (Table~\ref{tab:options}); all defaults reproduce
the conventional algorithm to machine precision. The filter of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:filter} is applied to the plane-wave coefficients from which
the selection Gram matrix is assembled, while a second, unfiltered set of
orbital values supplies the collocation matrices
$\varphi_n(\rr_\mu)$ returned to the solver; the surrogate therefore cannot
contaminate the fitted representation. The pair weights enter the Gram
diagonal and the gathered pivot columns as $N_L$ nonnegative separable
terms; positivity of the expansion preserves the positive semidefiniteness
of the selection matrix by the Schur product theorem, and the pivoted
factorization retains its unconditional numerical stability. The
re-ranking step operates on the Cholesky factor produced by the first
pass, so the exactness statement of Eq.~\eqref{eq:KPP} holds for any
combination of the preceding schemes: the three modifications compose.

For pseudopotentials with augmentation charges (ultrasoft and PAW), the
implementation appends atom-centered augmentation channels to the
interpolation basis after point selection, ranked by a
$\qq$-aware Coulomb norm. A Coulomb-metric point selection thus renders
the smooth and augmentation sectors of the auxiliary basis mutually
consistent.
